# 07 — Where a surname works, and where it does not

Analyses 01 and 05 give one national number and split it by caste. Neither says
*where* in India the name works.

**Finding.** The spread across states is much wider than the national figure
suggests: the same guess closes **67% of the gap in Assam and 0% in Haryana**.
The states are ordered by it, with each state's coverage printed beside the
result, because every figure here describes surnames that cleared outkast's
100-record disclosure floor and those cover between 3% and 19% of a state.

**What carries a state is one or two big names, not many.** Punjab has five
majority-Dalit surnames among its 25 commonest and closes 3% of the gap; Assam
has two and closes 67%, because one of them is `das` — a tenth of Assam's
retained surnames, 65% Dalit. Haryana has none at all: not one of its 36
retained surnames is majority Dalit.

**Not an explanation.** An earlier version tried to explain the spread by how
concentrated a state's names are. It does not hold — +0.02 against roll
concentration, −0.27 measured inside the extract — and the section saying so
stays in the note.

**The objection, tested.** The floor keeps a state's commonest names, and common
names are the uninformative ones, so a state retaining few names could look
uninformative by construction. Cutting every state to its top 25 names holds
that constant and the ordering survives.
